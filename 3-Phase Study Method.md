<b>**Phase 1 — Read the Notes**</b>

</td>
</tr>
</table>
 
I started by reading through my notes based on **AWS official documentation**.

I also used **Mohammed Khan's notes**, which were especially helpful during this phase because they were already structured around the exam topics. I didn't use them as something to memorize word-for-word. They were more like a roadmap that helped me understand what I was supposed to know.

The goal wasn't to memorize every service or definition on the first pass.

I just wanted to get familiar with the language of the exam.

Things like:

- What is RAG?
- What is inference?
- What is fine-tuning?
- What are embeddings?
- What does Amazon Bedrock actually do?
- When would I use one AWS AI service instead of another?

If something didn't make sense immediately, I didn't spend 30 minutes staring at the same paragraph.

I marked it mentally and kept going.

The first pass was really just:

> **"Let me see all of this once so it doesn't look completely unfamiliar later."**

My main resources for this phase were:

- AWS Skill Builder
- AWS Documentation
- My own notes
- Mohammed Khan's notes

You can find the resources I used [here](Resources%20%26%20Practice%20Tests.md).

<b>**Phase 2 — Practice**</b>

</td>
</tr>
</table>

After getting familiar with the concepts, I started doing practice questions.

This is where I started finding the gaps in my understanding.

I didn't want to simply memorize:

**Question → Answer → Next question**

Instead, I tried to understand:

**Why is this answer correct?** and **Why are the other answers wrong?**

I used practice questions to figure out what I needed to review.

This will challenge your mind to remember concepts/terms and how the questions will just confuse you with its wordings/structure.

<b>**Phase 3 — Watch** </b>

</td>
</tr>
</table>

Only after getting familiar with the concepts did I watch videos. You will not be bored since you already know the concepts, it will be more like someone telling a story about something you already knew. "Oh, I already saw this. Let me see how someone else explains it." And sometimes that second explanation was what finally made something click. This will really help especially to those who are visual learners.

At this point, it didn't feel like learning everything from scratch anymore. This is where I learned what inference really is, as it can be confusing with Fine-Tuning and RAG.
